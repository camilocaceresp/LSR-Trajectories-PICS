Why this review

A gap that needs closing

Surviving critical illness does not equate to recovery. The evidence exists — but it is fragmented, domain-specific, and never integrated into longitudinal estimates that can inform the next generation of clinical and Bayesian research.

01

Domains studied in isolation

Existing systematic reviews have examined physical function, cognition, depression, PTSD, HRQoL, and disability as separate literatures. No review has synthesised quantitative trajectories of change across all five PICS domains simultaneously from cohort data with multiple measurement points.

02

Trajectories, not point estimates

Most prior evidence captures point-prevalence at a single follow-up. This review pools the magnitude of change from ICU discharge through each pre-specified time window — generating the longitudinal picture the field has been missing.

03

Bayesian priors for future studies

The τ² estimates from this review will parameterise domain-specific informative priors for future Bayesian studies in adults recovering from critical illness. A living design ensures these priors are updated as new cohort evidence accumulates.

Objectives

What we set out to answer

One primary objective, six pre-specified secondary objectives.

To synthesise the longitudinal trajectories of physical function, cognition, mental health, disability, and health-related quality of life in adults who survived critical illness, from ICU discharge through at least three months of follow-up, using cohort studies with two or more measurement points.

SO — 01

Estimate the MD or SMD of change per domain and subdomain at each time point, depending on instrument homogeneity across studies.

SO — 02

Quantify between-study heterogeneity (I² and τ²) by domain and follow-up time point.

SO — 03

Map measurement instruments used across domains and subdomains.

SO — 04

Explore sources of variability through meta-regression: ICU type, illness severity, age, mechanical ventilation duration, and frailty at baseline.

SO — 05

Generate domain-specific informative priors for future Bayesian studies in adults recovering from critical illness.

SO — 06

Estimate the probability that recovery exceeds the MCID per domain and time point, using Bayesian meta-analysis with vague priors.

Statistical innovation

Two complementary Bayesian analyses

The Bayesian framework distinguishes this review from a standard meta-analysis. Both analyses are pre-specified and updated at each annual cycle.

Analysis 1 · Vague priors

Probability of clinically meaningful recovery

A Bayesian meta-analysis is performed for each domain and time point using vague priors (half-normal with scale 0.5 for τ). This analysis directly estimates the probability that the pooled change exceeds the instrument-specific MCID.

Where no validated MCID is available, SMD ≥ 0.5 is used as the reference threshold. Results are reported as posterior probabilities with 95% credible intervals.

Prior for τHalf-normal(0, 0.5)
OutputP(Δ > MCID)
FallbackSMD ≥ 0.5
Reported asPosterior prob. + 95% CrI
Analysis 2 · Informative priors

Prior derivation for future studies

The pooled MD or SMD and τ² from the frequentist meta-analyses parameterise domain-specific informative priors for future Bayesian studies. For each domain and time window:

θ ~ N(μpooled, σ²pooled + τ²)
μ_pooledMean trajectory of change
σ²_pooledVariance of pooled estimate
τ²Between-study heterogeneity
OutputTable by domain × time point
Both analyses connect directly to the living review update cycle — priors are revised at each annual search update as new cohort data are incorporated. This creates a continuously evolving evidence base that future trials and prognostic studies can use directly.
