The problem

Surviving critical illness does not equate to recovery. Post-intensive care syndrome (PICS) describes new or worsening impairments in physical, cognitive, or mental health that persist beyond hospital discharge. Existing systematic reviews have examined these domains in isolation — as separate literatures — limiting the ability to characterise the natural history of recovery and to generate integrated longitudinal estimates.

Our approach

  • Simultaneous synthesis of all five PICS domains
  • Cohort studies with ≥2 measurement points, baseline at ICU discharge
  • Random-effects meta-analysis (REML)
  • Two complementary Bayesian analyses: vague priors (P > MCID) and informative prior derivation
  • Living design with 12-month search update cycles
  • Semi-automated screening and data extraction (AI-assisted)

Statistical Methods

Analysis framework

Frequentist meta-analysis

  • Random-effects model, REML estimation (metafor)
  • MD when same instrument; SMD when different
  • Cochran's Q, I² with 95% uncertainty intervals, τ²
  • Meta-regression: age, MV duration, illness severity, ICU type, frailty, income level
  • Subgroup: design, ICU type, era, follow-up duration, instrument type
  • Sensitivity: low RoB restriction, <30% loss to follow-up

Bayesian analyses

  • Analysis 1: Vague priors (half-normal, scale 0.5 for τ) — estimates P(change > MCID) per domain and time point
  • Analysis 2: Informative prior derivation — θ ~ N(μ_pooled, σ²_pooled + τ²) — updated each annual cycle
  • Implemented via bayesmeta (R)
  • SMD ≥ 0.5 as fallback MCID threshold
  • 95% credible intervals reported
